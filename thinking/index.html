What guides my practice

The principles, questions and beliefs shaping how I approach education and learning design.

My approach to education has been shaped by years of working with students, lecturers and academic teams, and by increasingly finding myself involved in the design of learning experiences.

As I have moved into online education and developed my understanding of learning design, I have become more conscious of the decisions we make about how people learn. I have also become more aware of the importance of questioning established practices rather than simply accepting that something is the way it has always been done.

There are a few ideas that continue to guide my approach.

  1. 1Start with the student
  2. 2Make every element count
  3. 3Learning is about connection
  4. 4Technology must serve learning
  5. 5Respect what has come before, but remain willing to change

Principle 1

Start with the student

I believe education should be designed around the people experiencing it, not simply around institutional processes or the delivery of content.

Students are whole people. They have jobs, families, responsibilities and lives outside their studies. These realities influence how they engage with education, and our learning environments should recognise this.

I believe learning should be meaningful, accessible and rigorous. Making learning easier to navigate does not mean removing its challenge or value. It means identifying unnecessary barriers that get in the way.

“What does this actually mean for the student?”

Principle 2

Make every element count

I believe that every part of a learning experience should have a purpose.

Whether we are considering content, learning activities, assessment, administrative processes or the structure of an online environment, we should be able to explain why something is there and how it contributes to learning.

This means being prepared to question existing practices, challenge unnecessary complexity and consider whether there is a better way of doing things.

For me, good design isn't necessarily about adding more. Sometimes it is about making things clearer, more manageable and more meaningful.

Principle 3

Learning is about connection

I don't believe learning should be an isolated experience.

Finding your tribe

At The Animation School, I want students to have the opportunity to find their tribe: people who share their interests and passions, who understand their creative ambitions and with whom they can learn and grow.

This is particularly important when thinking about online education. We need to be deliberate about creating opportunities for students to connect, participate, share their work and learn from one another.

A learning environment should support more than the transfer of knowledge. It should also create a sense of belonging.

Principle 4

Technology must serve learning

Technology creates exciting opportunities for education, but its use needs to be purposeful.

I believe the educational need should determine the technology, not the other way around.

Introducing a new platform or tool does not automatically improve learning. The question should be:

  • What are we trying to achieve?
  • What do students and lecturers need?
  • Does the technology genuinely improve the experience?

As online education continues to evolve, I want to remain curious and open to new possibilities while being critical about how and why we use them.

Principle 5

Respect what has come before, but remain willing to change

I work in an institution with a strong history of practical, face-to-face animation education. I believe there is enormous value in the knowledge and experience that already exists within traditional approaches to teaching.

However, the world around education is changing. Students, technology and the industries they are preparing to enter are changing too.

We need to respect established practice while being willing to question it, experiment and explore new possibilities.

I have also come to recognise that meaningful change requires more than identifying a better approach. It requires collaboration, the ability to communicate the value of an idea and the influence to help others see what is possible.

Still developing.

These ideas are not a finished philosophy or a set of rules I have mastered. They are principles I am continuing to explore and apply as I develop my knowledge and experience in learning design.

I want to become more confident in the theory behind my decisions, more deliberate in evaluating learning experiences and better equipped to support others in developing their own practice.

Ultimately, I want to contribute to an education environment where students feel supported, connected and capable, and where every part of the learning experience has a purpose.
